I faved this photo when I took it. Something happened in my brain, like a puzzle piece _sliding into place_. I wanted to mentally park it in one of my main categories: either autism or bipolar. I have, after all, spent a decade detangling the mania of bipolar and only recently learned about the autism.

But it was perhaps the secret third option: **my self-worth**.

I look at the photo and recall the ease of my body riding a bike in the early evening warmth of July. A muscle tee, a sports bra, and jean shorts. Nothing exciting or new, so what was the puzzle-piece feeling? Maybe it was observing my body from a neutral stance. No forced self-love or attaching my worth to something I'll attain down the road.

No more sitting ducks. No more waiting.

Neutrality is such a balanced and welcoming friend that holds up no mirror or judgement. It's a big breath before the scaries... except the scary part never arrives. (FYI, the scaries were usually my eating disorder shouting.)

It does bring up another question, though: if hating yourself can't result in a positive outcome, and neither can forced self-love... what's the difference here? My autism brain likes the black-and-white aspect. 1+1 should equal 2, so why is it just as detrimental to my brain? It's essentially two radical sides of the same coin, and forcefully doing _anything_ is never going to work.

I assume this is an obvious statement to some, but it's news to me and my late-diagnosed brain. I'm perhaps a little embarrassed by this. The embarrassment, of course, stems from always having to perform or mask my autism in such a way that I go unnoticed, or not cause issues, at the very least.

But maybe I have stopped maintaining every single person's opinion of me... Somehow, in the past 18 months, the goal now is to _actively_ disappoint people I wouldn't trust with my safety and/or a potato gun. Within that is also being misunderstood—my absolute worst fear as an autistic person—because there is some trauma, for sure, I have not started to deal with yet.

I aim to be a cosmic inconvenience for mediocre folk. It's a post-people-pleaser path, and it's rocky.

But there you are, self. There you finally are. I guess, be who you needed as a kid kind of worked.
